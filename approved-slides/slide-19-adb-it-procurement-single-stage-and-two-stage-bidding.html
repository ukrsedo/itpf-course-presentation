Session 1 Setting the Scene

ADB IT Procurement: Single-Stage and Two-Stage Bidding

When to use it

Straightforward IT products and services.

The purchaser can define the requirements before inviting final bids.

Selection follows the complexity of the subject.

Evaluation approach

Lowest evaluated price: the guide identifies the goods bidding document where this achieves value for money among substantially responsive bids.

Quality matters: the IT products and services bidding document supports point scoring to give technical quality greater importance.

Envelope variants

One-envelope
The main procedure for most ADB-financed procurement.

Two-envelope
Technical and administrative evaluation takes place without reference to price.

When to use it

Complex business applications and transformational IT projects.

Extensive development, integration, customisation or technical services.

Start with business and functional requirements.

Stage 1: technical development

Invite technical proposals without prices.

Use structured technical discussions to clarify conformity, gaps and proposed solutions.

Refine the specifications and bidding documents where needed. Document the understanding with each bidder.

Stage 2: final bids

Invite sufficiently responsive bidders to submit:

Revised technical proposals

Financial proposals

Evaluate the complete final bids against the agreed requirements.

Source: ADB, User Guide for Procurement of Information Technology Products and Services (December 2021), pp. 5–6
