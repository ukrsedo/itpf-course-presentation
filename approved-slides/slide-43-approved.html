Waterfall, Incremental and Agile Delivery Models

Waterfall delivers value only at cutover, incremental delivers in staged releases, and agile delivers in short iterations with frequent feedback and reprioritisation.

Commercial model by delivery approach

Waterfall
Commercial structure
Usually fixed price or milestone-based, with a defined scope baseline and formal change requests.
Contracting focus
Detailed SOW, milestone payments, final acceptance testing, warranty terms, and tight change control.
Incremental
Commercial structure
Phased pricing by release, work package or tranche, with optionality for later stages.
Contracting focus
Release-based acceptance criteria, stage gates, phased renew/stop decisions, and pricing tied to delivered functionality.
Agile
Commercial structure
Often time-and-materials, sprint capacity, product team subscription, or capped T&M with backlog prioritisation.
Contracting focus
Team composition, backlog ownership, sprint review rights, governance cadence, and exit flexibility.

What procurement should negotiate

Area
Negotiation implication
Pricing basis
Use fixed price for stable scope, phased pricing for incremental releases, and capacity-based pricing for agile teams.
Acceptance
Waterfall needs detailed final acceptance; incremental needs release-by-release acceptance; agile needs sprint review and definition-of-done governance.
Change control
The more change is expected, the less useful rigid CR mechanics become. Agile contracts need backlog and prioritisation rules.
Risk allocation
In waterfall, suppliers can carry more schedule/scope risk. In agile, risk shifts toward buyer governance, prioritisation and decision speed.
  • Waterfall: negotiate milestones, deliverables, warranties, defect correction periods and remedies for late cutover.
  • Incremental: negotiate stage gates, rollback rights, options to pause after a release and transparent pricing for future increments.
  • Agile: negotiate named roles, team continuity, backlog ownership, reporting cadence, sprint demos and termination support.
Bottom line: the contract should not fight the delivery model. An agile project wrapped in a rigid waterfall contract will create governance friction and commercial disputes.

Iron Triangle

Core lesson: project constraints are interdependent. Delivery method changes how the trade-offs are managed, but the trade-offs do not disappear.

Waterfall Usually fixes scope. Any material change moves time, cost or both. Contract focus: defined deliverables, milestones and change control.
Agile Usually fixes time and cost through sprint cadence and team capacity. Scope flexes through backlog reprioritisation.
Executive rule: decide early what is truly fixed — scope, time or cost — then align the delivery model, the contract structure and the governance model to that choice.
