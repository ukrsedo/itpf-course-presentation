Technology Procurement Framework — Cloud

Cloud economics depend on both consumption discipline and the commercial structure around it.
Demand Management
Commercial Negotiation
Cost Management
Spend Visibility & Control
  • Spend visibility by account, workload, service and region
  • Budgets, alerts, forecasts and anomaly monitoring
  • Tags / labels, cost centres, showback or chargeback
  • Track non-production, DR, backup and data-transfer spend
Toolkit: billing reports • budgets & alerts • tagging • allocation dashboards
Cost Optimization
Reduce inefficient consumption
  • Rightsize compute, databases and storage
  • Stop idle resources; schedule non-production environments
  • Use autoscaling, serverless and storage tiering where appropriate
  • Remove orphaned resources and redesign expensive workloads
Toolkit: rightsizing • autoscaling • scheduling • lifecycle rules • architecture changes
Commercial Levers
Improve the deal around optimized demand
  • Challenge commitment level and negotiate reallocation flexibility
  • Check discount coverage, including future services and marketplace spend
  • Challenge support model and professional-service pricing
  • Protect exit/data rights; address public/private SLA and hidden-cost exposure
Toolkit: commitments • discount scope • support • exit rights • SLA / cost structure
Do not lock in a cloud commitment before the underlying demand has been challenged and optimized.

Cloud commitments: buying a discount while accepting demand risk

A commitment exchanges flexibility for better commercial terms. Its value depends on whether the organisation can consume the committed amount through eligible workloads during the agreed period.

The decision therefore requires scenarios for lower, expected and higher consumption. Migration delays, decommissioning and technical optimisation can all affect the forecast.
