Session 5 Source-to-Contract

Incomplete Contracts and the Hold-up Problem

Why contracts remain incomplete

No contract can specify every action for every possible future state. Uncertainty leaves gaps, discretion and decision rights.

When one party makes a relationship-specific investment, those gaps may allow the counterparty to renegotiate after dependency has been created.
1
Specific investmentThe investment has limited value outside this relationship.
2
DependencySwitching becomes expensive or destroys the investment.
3
Opportunistic renegotiationThe stronger party seeks to capture more value after lock-in.
4
UnderinvestmentAnticipating hold-up, the buyer may reject an otherwise valuable investment.

How procurement can reduce the risk

Vertical integrationBring the critical capability in-house where market dependency is unacceptable.
Long-term contractual protectionFix red-flag terms, decision rights, price mechanisms, change control and exit rights.
Revenue or risk sharingAlign incentives so both parties gain from the investment’s success.
Relational governanceUse escalation, transparency and joint decision-making where every contingency cannot be written down.
The objective is not to make the contract complete. It is to make opportunism less attractive and adaptation more governable.

Scenario assumptions

Buyer’s specialised investment£100k
Incremental benefit if the project continues£180k
Supplier’s post-investment price increase£70k
Supplier’s loss if the buyer exits£40k

All figures are incremental and exclude the original contract price.

Knowing these possible outcomes, should the buyer invest?
Step 1 — Buyer invests £100k. The supplier then chooses whether to honour the deal or demand an opportunistic £70k increase.

Supplier honours the original economics

The project continues and creates the intended benefit.

Buyer: +£80k
Supplier: £0 extra

Supplier demands an extra £70k after lock-in

Step 2 — Buyer must respond: accept the increase or refuse it and terminate/switch.

Buyer response to opportunistic price increase

A. Accept and continue

£180k benefit − £100k investment − £70k increase
Buyer: +£10k
Supplier: +£70k
The buyer preserves the project but rewards the supplier’s opportunistic behaviour.

B. Refuse and terminate/switch

The specialised investment is stranded; the supplier loses the account and future margin.
Buyer: −£100k
Supplier: −£40k
Refusal may protect precedent and credibility, but both parties destroy value.
Backward-induction lesson: if the buyer expects a post-investment price increase and is unwilling to reward it, the rational ex-ante decision may be do not invest (Buyer £0; Supplier £0). The fear of hold-up therefore causes underinvestment even though cooperation could create £80k of buyer value.
