Session 5 Source-to-Contract

AI Procurement Contracts

Case: US defence AI procurement guidance

The guidance turns a policy for accurate, ideologically neutral AI into three procurement templates: A selects supplier disclosures, B requests them in the tender, and C makes compliance an ongoing contract obligation. Truth-seeking means factual accuracy and acknowledgement of uncertainty. Ideological neutrality means avoiding manipulation in favour of ideological positions.

For LLM purchases, suppliers provide their developer’s acceptable-use policy, model/system/data cards explaining training, risks and evaluation results, user guidance and a feedback mechanism. Buyers may request further bias evaluations. Missing required information may make a bid ineligible. The table summarises the ongoing controls.

  • Testing methodology: agree representative tasks, test data, sampling, model settings, testing frequency and responsibility for validation.
  • Objective criteria: set measurable quality thresholds, severity levels and rules distinguishing isolated errors from contractual breaches.
  • Hallucination control: define fabricated or unsupported factual claims, an SLA and measurement method, correction deadlines, supplier-funded retraining and buyer reacceptance.
  • Dispute resolution: preserve evidence and buyer audit rights, independent verification, escalation and a process for disputed findings.
  • Change controls and reacceptance: define material changes, advance notice, approval rights, regression tests, renewed acceptance and rollback.
  • Data and IP: define licensed scope, ownership and permitted use, confidentiality, retention, deletion and restrictions on supplier training with buyer data.
  • Service and security: agree availability, latency, access controls, monitoring, incident response and obligations for subcontractors.
  • Liability and exit: allocate IP and data risks, remedies and termination rights, and secure portability, transition support and deletion confirmation.
