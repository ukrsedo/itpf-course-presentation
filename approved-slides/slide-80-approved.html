Session 5 Source-to-Contract

AI Procurement Contracts

1

Licensing scope and definitions

Define exactly what is licensed: models, APIs, datasets, inputs, outputs and any fine-tuned artefacts.

2

Vendor data-use rights

Restrict the supplier from using buyer inputs, outputs or metadata for training, analytics or other customers without explicit consent.

3

Confidentiality and data governance

Specify data location, retention, deletion, access controls, sub-processors and treatment of proprietary or sensitive information.

4

Ownership and permitted use

Clarify ownership and permitted use of inputs, outputs, prompts, embeddings, configurations and generated content.

5

IP infringement and indemnity

Allocate risk for claims arising from training data, model operation or generated outputs; define warranties, filters and defence obligations.

Buyer question: What data or intellectual property could the supplier reuse, retain or expose after the contract ends?
1

Service levels

Define measurable commitments for availability, latency, throughput, support and recovery.

2

Accuracy and quality

Use agreed benchmarks, test cases and acceptance criteria where output quality is material to the use case.

3

Warranties and disclaimers

Separate normal AI limitations from explicit performance promises. Avoid relying on general marketing claims.

4

Change control and versioning

Require notice, testing rights and remediation for material changes to models, APIs, safety controls or functionality.

5

Validation and audit rights

Preserve the buyer’s ability to test outputs, inspect relevant evidence and verify performance against the agreed method.

6

Corrective action

Define service credits, retraining, reconfiguration, rollback, replacement or termination when performance repeatedly fails.

Important: “Hallucination” or accuracy SLAs are only workable when the contract defines the use case, test set, sampling method, tolerance and validation owner.
1

Security and monitoring

Set requirements for access control, incident response, logging, model abuse prevention and monitoring of material risks.

2

Compliance and permitted uses

Allocate responsibility for privacy, sector regulation, export controls, records management and prohibited applications.

3

Liability and indemnification

Set meaningful caps and exceptions for data breaches, confidentiality failures, IP claims and regulatory exposure.

4

Insurance

Require suitable coverage for cyber, professional liability and relevant legal or regulatory claims where available.

5

Portability and exit

Secure retrieval of data and artefacts, transition support, deletion confirmation and continuity during migration.

6

Termination and disputes

Define exit triggers for repeated performance failure, security incidents, legal claims or material unilateral changes.

Core lesson: AI contracting is not a separate legal universe; it extends established controls for data, service performance, change, liability and exit to model-specific risks.
