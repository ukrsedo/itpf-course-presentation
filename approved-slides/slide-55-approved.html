AI-Enabled Sourcing Risk

AI can accelerate analysis and communication, but it also creates new evidence, governance and accountability risks.

Where procurement is exposed

Unsupported claimsAI may produce plausible but false facts, benchmarks, supplier comparisons or contract interpretations.
Supplier-generated contentProposals, references and technical statements may be AI-assisted, making authorship and validation less transparent.
Hidden limitationsModels may use incomplete, outdated or biased information without clearly showing what is missing.
Confidentiality leakageTender data, prices, evaluation notes or personal information may enter tools outside approved controls.
Inconsistent evaluationAutomated scoring can reproduce bias or apply criteria unevenly when prompts, data or model versions change.

Procurement controls

Validate evidenceRequire named sources, direct links, dates and verification before using AI-supported claims in decisions.
Assign accountabilityKeep a named human owner for supplier evaluation, recommendations and external communication.
Control dataDefine which procurement information may enter AI tools and use only approved environments.
Audit the processRetain prompts, outputs, model versions, scoring logic and decision records for material sourcing events.
Contract for transparencySet supplier obligations for AI-use disclosure, data handling, correction, monitoring and liability.
Use AI to support procurement judgement—not to replace evidence, governance or accountable decision-making.
